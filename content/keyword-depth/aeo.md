## REPLACE/REFINE the AEO vs SEO section

### AEO vs SEO: where they overlap and where they differ

AEO and SEO share much of the same foundation. Both depend on useful content, accessible pages, clear entities, credible evidence and a technically sound website.

The difference is the retrieval experience.

Traditional SEO is primarily concerned with earning visibility in search results and attracting a user to a page. Answer Engine Optimisation also considers whether an AI system can identify, interpret and use your information when constructing an answer.

That changes some of the work. Definitions need to be clear. Relationships between products, services, people and topics need to be explicit. Important claims need evidence. Useful answers should not be buried beneath unnecessary copy.

But AEO is not a replacement for SEO. A strong search foundation still matters because many of the sources answer engines use are discovered, crawled and understood through the same web ecosystem.

For B2B businesses, the practical question is not “SEO or AEO?” It is whether buyers can find, understand and validate the business across both search and AI-assisted research.

## REFINE the services/method bridge

### AEO services from audit to measurement

Effective AEO services connect four things: what buyers ask, what your website says, what machines can interpret and what can actually be measured.

Commview's approach combines:

- visibility and citation auditing
- buyer-question and topic mapping
- content restructuring and content-gap analysis
- technical and entity improvements
- source and evidence review
- repeatable citation and answer tracking
- measurement alongside SEO and commercial outcomes

The deeper [Answer Engine Optimization services](/growth/aeo/services) page explains how those workstreams are delivered.

The objective is not to manufacture mentions. It is to make the business easier to understand, retrieve and validate, then measure whether visibility changes over time.

## REFINE the B2B section

### AEO for B2B starts before the sales conversation

B2B buyers often need to understand a category, define requirements, compare approaches and validate suppliers before they speak to sales.

That makes B2B AEO particularly relevant to the questions that shape a shortlist.

A buyer might ask an AI system to explain a technical category, compare two approaches, identify providers for a specific use case, summarise implementation considerations or surface evidence about a supplier.

The opportunity is therefore broader than getting the company name into an answer. The business needs useful, supportable material across the subjects that influence the buying decision.

That can include category definitions, service and product pages, comparisons, implementation guidance, customer evidence, expert material and clear answers to recurring buyer questions.

The role of AEO is to make that expertise easier for both people and machines to find and interpret.
