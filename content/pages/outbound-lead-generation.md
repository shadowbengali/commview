## Outbound is not a volume problem

The old outbound equation was simple: give an SDR a large list, increase the number of touches and hope enough people respond.

Technology made that model easier to scale. It did not necessarily make it better.

AI and automation can now research accounts, enrich contacts, surface signals, draft context, sequence activity and remove much of the administration that once consumed an SDR's day. That creates an opportunity to redesign outbound around relevance rather than simply automate more noise.

Before we decide how many emails to send or calls to make, we want to know:

- Which accounts actually fit?
- Why might they care now?
- Who inside the account matters?
- What problem can we credibly open a conversation around?
- What signal tells us to prioritise one account over another?
- What should happen when somebody engages?
- When is the conversation ready for an AE?

If those answers are weak, adding more SDR capacity usually scales the weakness.

## B2B outbound lead generation starts before the first message

A campaign is only as good as the thinking underneath it.

### ICP and account selection

We define the characteristics that make an account commercially attractive, then turn the ICP into an addressable account universe.

That can include firmographic fit, sector, geography, company stage, technology, commercial characteristics and the problems your proposition is actually good at solving.

The point is not to build the biggest list. It is to make the list defensible.

### Signals and timing

Not every target account deserves the same priority today.

Hiring, funding, leadership changes, expansion, product launches, technology changes, website behaviour, content engagement, intent data and other observable events can create useful context.

Signals are not proof that somebody wants to buy. They are reasons to look more closely and decide whether outreach has a credible "why now?"

### People and buying groups

B2B decisions rarely belong to a single contact.

We identify the people likely to influence the problem, the decision and the implementation rather than building a list around one job title.

That gives outbound somewhere to go when the first contact is not the buyer.

## Build the outbound system, not another sequence

Outbound usually crosses several systems and several people. If they are disconnected, the prospect feels it.

Our outbound operating model is:

**ICP → ACCOUNTS → SIGNALS → CONTACTS → CONTEXT → OUTREACH → CONVERSATION → QUALIFICATION → AE**

### 01. Target

Define the ICP, account universe, buying group and prioritisation logic.

### 02. Understand

Bring together account context, relevant signals and the commercial reason for outreach.

### 03. Engage

Coordinate email, LinkedIn and phone around a coherent proposition rather than treating each channel as a separate campaign.

### 04. Converse

Use people where people add value: understanding the response, asking the next question, handling nuance and deciding whether there is a real opportunity.

### 05. Qualify and route

Capture what has been learned and move the conversation to the right next step with the context intact.

### 06. Learn

Feed objections, replies, meetings, opportunity quality and closed outcomes back into targeting and messaging.

Outbound becomes a learning system, not a monthly activity report.

## Multi-channel outbound campaigns

Cold email is a channel. LinkedIn is a channel. Calling is a channel.

**Outbound is the system connecting them.**

We design the channel mix around the buyer, the market and the proposition.

### Email

Useful for opening relevant conversations at scale, testing propositions and creating structured follow-up. Deliverability, data quality and message relevance matter before clever copy does.

### LinkedIn

Useful for account research, context, visibility and person-to-person engagement. We do not treat connection-request spam as a strategy.

### Phone

Calling can still be one of the fastest ways to understand whether the problem, timing and contact are real. We use it selectively and in context rather than judging an SDR by raw dial volume.

### Signals and inbound behaviour

Where first-party or third-party signals are available, they can change account priority and the next action. Outbound should not ignore evidence simply because the account has not filled in a form.

The channels should share context. The buyer should not experience four unrelated campaigns from the same company.

## AI changes what an SDR should spend time doing

AI does not remove the need for sales development. It changes where human time is valuable.

Research, enrichment, summarisation, account context, prioritisation support, first-draft personalisation, CRM administration and workflow orchestration can increasingly be assisted or automated.

That means an SDR should be able to spend more time on the work that benefits from judgement:

**Is this account genuinely relevant?**

**What does this response actually mean?**

**Which question should I ask next?**

**Is there enough evidence of a problem to involve an AE?**

**Who else matters inside the account?**

The modern SDR becomes a conversational and qualification layer inside a wider GTM system, not a human API between a database and a sequencing tool.

## From outbound response to AE without the broken handoff

A booked meeting is not automatically pipeline.

The handoff needs to carry what the organisation has learned: the account context, original signal, conversation history, people involved, problem, timing, objections and agreed next step.

We do not believe every marketing response needs to become an MQL that gets thrown across a departmental line.

Inbound behaviour, outbound engagement, product signals, account fit and human conversations can contribute to the same account picture.

The AE should enter when an experienced salesperson adds value to a developing commercial conversation, not because a lead score crossed an arbitrary number.

## What we measure in an outbound campaign

Calls made, emails sent and connection requests are operating metrics. They tell us whether activity happened.

They do not tell us whether outbound is working.

We build measurement around the progression towards commercial value:

**Target accounts reached → meaningful engagement → positive responses → qualified conversations → attended meetings → opportunities → pipeline → revenue**

Depending on the sales cycle, we also look at:

- account coverage
- contact coverage within buying groups
- response and positive-response rate
- conversation quality
- qualification rate
- meeting attendance
- meeting-to-opportunity conversion
- pipeline value
- cost per qualified conversation
- cost per opportunity
- time to opportunity
- objections and disqualification reasons

The further the data gets towards revenue, the more useful it becomes.

## When outbound is the wrong answer

Outbound cannot rescue every commercial problem.

It is a poor place to start if you cannot describe the customer, the proposition has not earned any evidence, the economics cannot support the cost of creating a conversation, or nobody is able to handle the opportunities the campaign creates.

Sometimes the work before outbound is [positioning](/positioning).

Sometimes it is defining the [ideal customer profile](/ideal-customer-profile-workshop).

Sometimes the business needs broader [B2B lead generation](/growth/b2b-lead-generation) or [demand generation](/growth/demand-generation).

We would rather diagnose that before building an outbound machine around the wrong problem.

## Outbound lead generation FAQ

### Does cold outbound still work?

Yes, but "does outbound work?" is too broad a question. Performance depends on the market, proposition, account selection, data, timing, channel, message and follow-up. Sending more generic outreach because automation made it cheap is not the same as having an effective outbound system. We test whether the motion creates commercially useful conversations.

### Should we hire SDRs or outsource outbound?

Hire internally when the motion is understood, the workload is persistent and you want the capability permanently inside the business. Outsourcing can make sense when you need to test a motion, enter a market or add capability without building the whole function first. Commview can also work with existing SDRs rather than replacing them.

### How much of outbound can AI automate?

AI can assist research, enrichment, account summaries, signal interpretation, personalisation drafts, sequencing, CRM administration and reporting. We would not hand qualification and commercial judgement to automation by default. The objective is to remove low-value work so people spend more time on relevant conversations and better decisions.

### Is cold calling still worth doing in B2B?

It can be. A phone conversation can expose fit, timing, objections and buying context faster than a long automated sequence. We use calling where the economics, audience and proposition justify it. The point is not maximum dial volume. It is using the right channel to create and develop a useful conversation.

### What is the difference between outbound lead generation and B2B lead generation?

B2B lead generation is the broader discipline of creating and capturing potential commercial opportunities across channels. Outbound lead generation specifically identifies target accounts and proactively approaches them. Commview's broader [B2B lead generation service](/growth/b2b-lead-generation) covers the wider channel mix; this service focuses on the outbound motion.
