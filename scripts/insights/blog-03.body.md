## Growth doesn't slow. Something constrains it.

A familiar pattern appears in B2B scale-ups.

The company finds a market. The proposition works. A channel starts producing. Sales learns how to close it. Revenue climbs.

Then it becomes harder.

The same activity creates less pipeline. Customer acquisition cost rises. Conversion softens. Sales says the leads are worse. Marketing asks for more budget. Leadership starts looking for a new channel.

The instinct is to fix growth with more growth activity.

That can be exactly the wrong response.

Before spending another pound, work out which constraint you actually have.

I use four broad failure modes.

### Channel saturation

The audience is still there and the proposition still works, but your existing acquisition route is producing diminishing returns.

### Market saturation

You are reaching the limit of the segment you currently serve effectively.

### Execution saturation

Demand exists, but your organisation is no longer converting it efficiently because process, capacity, measurement or coordination has become the bottleneck.

### Product ceiling

The market opportunity exists, but the product or proposition cannot support the next stage of growth.

These can look remarkably similar in a board report.

The fix for each is completely different.

## Channel saturation vs market saturation vs execution saturation

Start with trends, not snapshots.

### Channel saturation

Look for rising acquisition cost inside a channel while downstream conversion remains broadly healthy.

For example, paid search may still bring the right buyers, but you are paying more to reach the same finite pool.

The wrong response is to assume the entire market has stopped growing.

The question becomes whether another channel can reach the same ICP economically.

### Market saturation

Here, the issue is broader.

You may still generate leads, but there are fewer genuinely new high-fit accounts available in the segment. Frequency increases. Branded demand stops growing. Outbound lists begin recycling the same organisations.

This is when simply adding another acquisition channel can disappoint. You have changed the route, not the addressable opportunity.

### Execution saturation

This is underestimated.

Perhaps Marketing creates more demand than Sales can properly work. Perhaps response times have increased. Perhaps qualification criteria changed without anyone noticing. Maybe campaigns launch slowly because every asset requires six approvals.

Demand exists, but the machine cannot process it.

Adding budget can make this worse.

### Product ceiling

The product may work beautifully for the segment that got you here but struggle outside it.

Enterprise prospects need controls you do not have. Smaller customers cannot reach value quickly enough. International buyers need localisation. A core integration becomes mandatory.

That is not a campaign problem.

## When you've maxed out your ICP

One of the most dangerous moments in growth is when the current ICP is still profitable but no longer large enough to support the next target.

Nothing appears catastrophically broken.

You still win customers.

You still generate pipeline.

But each incremental pound becomes harder to turn into growth.

Before expanding the ICP, look at the evidence.

Which segment has the best win rate?

Which has the shortest sales cycle?

Which produces the strongest retention or expansion?

Where is your current penetration genuinely high?

Which adjacent segment already appears in your customer base without heavy customisation?

The answer is not always "go enterprise".

Moving upmarket can increase contract value while also increasing sales-cycle length, procurement complexity, implementation requirements and product expectations.

Moving downmarket can increase volume while damaging economics through support and churn.

The second segment needs its own logic.

## The second growth engine playbook

A second growth engine can take several forms.

A new customer segment.

A new geography.

A new product.

A partner motion.

A different acquisition channel.

An expansion motion into existing customers.

The mistake is launching several at once.

Choose one hypothesis.

For example:

> We believe our product can solve the same operational problem for larger multi-site businesses, and that the higher contract value will justify the additional sales complexity.

Then define what has to be true.

Can the product support the segment?

Can you reach the buyer?

Does the problem carry enough urgency?

Can Sales navigate the buying process?

Can you deliver without destroying margin?

What evidence would make you stop?

For the first few months, the objective is not maximum revenue. It is learning whether the engine can become repeatable.

That requires different metrics from the mature core business.

## What not to do when growth flattens

### Hire more salespeople before finding the constraint

More capacity helps when capacity is the problem.

If the existing team lacks enough good opportunities, more people simply divide the same pipeline.

### Discount your way through weaker conversion

Discounting can temporarily improve close rates while hiding a deeper value or targeting problem.

If the buyer does not see enough value, changing the price does not necessarily fix the proposition.

### Copy the competitor's channel mix

You can see where competitors advertise.

You cannot see their economics, conversion quality, existing brand demand, partner relationships or internal capability.

Copying visible activity is not a growth strategy.

## Diagnose before you accelerate

The practical sequence is simple.

First, establish **where** growth changed.

Then establish **when**.

Then look for what changed at the same time.

Segment the problem by customer, channel, stage and product.

Do not average everything together.

A company can have healthy top-of-funnel growth and a collapsing close rate. Another can have excellent conversion but insufficient demand. A third can have both and still struggle because retention has deteriorated.

All three can report "growth has flattened".

Only one should buy more leads.
