## How ICPs drift

Most ideal customer profiles do not become useless because somebody wrote them badly.

They become useless because the business moves.

Sales closes an unexpected customer. That customer is worth decent money, so similar prospects start getting attention. Marketing notices the wins and adjusts targeting. Product starts hearing feature requests from the new segment. Six months later, the pipeline contains more of them.

Nobody formally changed the ICP.

But the ICP changed anyway.

This happens particularly easily in growing B2B companies because revenue is persuasive. If somebody wants to buy, saying no feels ridiculous.

The problem appears later.

Sales cycles get longer. Custom requirements increase. Onboarding becomes harder. Support costs rise. Product gets pulled in several directions. Marketing has to write for audiences with different problems.

Eventually the company is serving several different markets without consciously deciding to do so.

An ICP should prevent that drift.

Not by banning every customer outside a rigid definition, but by making the trade-offs visible.

## The best-customers analysis

If your ICP feels theoretical, do not start with another workshop.

Start with customers.

Take roughly 20 of your strongest accounts. "Strongest" should not simply mean highest revenue. Look for a combination of commercial value and quality of fit.

For each account, capture six things:

1. Company characteristics, such as size, sector and geography.
2. The situation that caused them to buy.
3. The problem they were trying to solve.
4. How long they took to buy.
5. How successfully they adopted or used what you sold.
6. Their commercial quality, including margin, retention, expansion or support burden where relevant.

Then look for patterns.

Imagine a software company believes its ICP is "UK businesses with 50 to 500 employees".

That is barely an ICP.

Its best-customer analysis might reveal something much more useful:

> Multi-site service businesses with 100 to 300 employees, a small central operations team, an existing manual process that becomes painful after expansion, and a senior operations leader already under pressure to standardise it.

Now you have something Marketing can target, Sales can recognise and Product can understand.

The most useful part is often the **situation**, not the industry label.

Two companies with identical firmographics can have completely different buying propensity because one has a painful reason to change and the other does not.

## Signals your ICP has drifted

No single metric proves ICP drift. Look for combinations.

### Sales cycles are getting longer

This can mean many things, but one possibility is that you are selling to organisations that require more education, more stakeholders or more product adaptation than your strongest customers.

### Win rates are falling while lead volume is healthy

Before blaming Sales or lead quality, segment the win rate.

You may discover that your historical core segment still converts well while newer segments do not.

### Average deal size is falling

You may be attracting companies that like the proposition but cannot justify the value at your target price.

That is not necessarily a pricing problem.

### Product requests are becoming less coherent

If one group wants simplicity, another wants enterprise controls and another wants bespoke integrations, Product may be experiencing a targeting problem downstream.

### Your best salespeople quietly ignore the ICP

Pay attention to this.

Good salespeople often develop pattern recognition before the organisation updates its documentation.

If the team repeatedly says, "Those companies never close", investigate the evidence rather than forcing compliance with an old profile.

## Rebuilding an ICP Sales and Marketing both use

A 30-slide persona deck will not fix this.

The ICP needs to be operational.

I would structure it around three layers.

### Firmographic fit

The basic boundaries.

Sector. Size. Geography. Business model. Technology environment where relevant.

Useful, but not enough.

### Behavioural fit

What does a good prospect actually do?

Perhaps they have already tried to solve the problem manually. Maybe they are hiring around the issue. Perhaps they use an adjacent technology. Maybe multiple people have started researching the category.

These behaviours can be much more predictive than company size alone.

### Situational fit

Why now?

A new regulation. Rapid growth. A leadership change. Geographic expansion. A major customer requirement. A cost-reduction target. A failed internal project.

This is where ICP work becomes commercially useful.

A company can fit every demographic field and still have no reason to buy.

## Keep the ICP alive

Ownership matters.

Marketing should not own the ICP alone.

Neither should Sales.

Product should not be absent from it.

Create a lightweight review using actual evidence. Once a month or once a quarter, depending on sales volume, look at:

- recently won accounts
- recently lost accounts
- sales-cycle changes
- expansion and churn
- common objections
- product requests
- support burden
- new buying triggers

Ask one simple question:

> Has the evidence changed who we should pursue?

If yes, update the ICP deliberately.

That is much healthier than allowing it to drift accidentally.

## What to do if you are targeting the wrong people

Do not swing the pendulum overnight.

First, identify which segment produces the strongest combination of conversion, value and customer success.

Then compare your current pipeline and spend against it.

You may find that the issue is not the entire ICP. It could be one channel attracting a poor-fit audience. It could be outbound targeting an outdated list. It could be positioning that appeals to smaller companies than your product is designed for.

Over the next 90 days:

**Tighten targeting.** Reduce spend and effort against clearly weak segments.

**Change qualification.** Give Sales explicit positive and negative fit signals.

**Align messaging.** Make the strongest customer's problem much more obvious in your proposition.

**Protect Product.** Stop treating one-off requests from weak-fit accounts as roadmap evidence.

**Measure by segment.** Lead volume alone will hide whether the shift is working.

An ICP is useful when it changes decisions.

If everyone can agree with it and nobody has to say no because of it, it probably is not specific enough.
